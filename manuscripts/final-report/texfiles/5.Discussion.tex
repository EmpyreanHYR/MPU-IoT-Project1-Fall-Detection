\section{Discussion}

The experiments reveal different behavior at the window and video levels. UniMamba and the Transformer encoder form stronger local decision boundaries on several window metrics, whereas MaskedBiMamba produces the best mean video F1 and false-alarm rate. One possible explanation is that the forward and backward branches use temporal context on both sides of an observation, while quality-weighted pooling reduces the influence of low-confidence frames. Training-time masking may further encourage stable evidence when short portions of a sequence are missing. These mechanisms need not improve every individual window; instead, they may make positive evidence more consistent when window predictions are aggregated over a video. This interpretation is consistent with the observed ranking but requires the planned ablation study for component-level confirmation.

The result also highlights why accuracy alone is insufficient for an IoT alert system. A detector can recognize most fall events while still producing too many false notifications during long non-fall periods. MaskedBiMamba retains high event recall while giving the lowest mean false-alarm burden among the evaluated models, but the absolute rate remains too high for unattended deployment. Validation-calibrated thresholds, temporal hysteresis, and hard-negative training are therefore practical next steps.

The edge--cloud architecture complements the temporal model by keeping routine RGB processing near the camera and transmitting derived pose observations. This supports privacy and lowers communication volume, while the cloud provides persistent state and a dashboard shared across clients. The current prototype demonstrates the required processing chain, but its engineering value must ultimately be supported by measured latency, throughput, resource use, and recovery behavior.
