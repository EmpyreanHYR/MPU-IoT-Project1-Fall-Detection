\section{Discussion}

The completed primary experiment reveals a trade-off rather than one uniformly dominant model. The Transformer encoder forms the strongest local decision boundary, with the highest window F1 and AUROC, and also gives the highest video F1 and event recall. MaskedBiMamba instead has the highest window precision and the lowest false-alarm rate. Its video F1 is within 0.0054 of the Transformer mean, but its event recall is lower. Thus, the proposed model is better characterized as a precision- and robustness-oriented alternative than as the overall accuracy winner.

The ablations clarify which mechanisms are consistent with that behavior. Removing bidirectionality or temporal attention lowers both window and video F1 and increases false alarms, supporting their inclusion. The evidence for the other mechanisms is mixed. Removing frame masking or quality-weighted pooling improves some clean-set means, while confidence input reduces the false-alarm rate and improves video F1 despite not maximizing window F1. These results caution against attributing all gains to the complete architecture and suggest that the pooling and masking settings should be tuned against the intended deployment cost rather than retained by default.

Missing-observation tests provide the clearest advantage for MaskedBiMamba. Under 30\% random frame loss, its primary window F1 changes only from 0.6395 to 0.6276, whereas TCNTE declines from 0.6255 to 0.4361. The same pattern transfers to Le2i. This establishes a difference between the two complete models under this perturbation. It does not isolate the cause: the ablations were evaluated on clean data, so the missing-frame advantage cannot be assigned independently to masking, attention, or bidirectionality. In contrast, both models deteriorate under random joint removal and complete lower-body loss, showing that temporal redundancy cannot fully replace missing spatial evidence.

The result also highlights why accuracy alone is insufficient for an IoT alert system. A detector can recognize most fall events while still producing too many false notifications during long non-fall periods. MaskedBiMamba gives the lowest mean false-alarm burden among the five primary models, but this offline rate cannot be equated with real notifications per non-fall monitoring hour. It nevertheless motivates a prospective continuous non-fall trial before unattended use. Validation-calibrated thresholds, temporal hysteresis, hard-negative training, and evaluation with a deployment-specific alarm cost are therefore practical next steps.

The edge--cloud architecture complements the temporal model by keeping routine RGB processing near the camera and transmitting derived pose observations. This supports privacy and lowers communication volume, while the cloud provides persistent state and a dashboard shared across clients. The source code and recorded deployment notes describe the required processing chain; the interface capture in this revision verifies the local application only, but its engineering value must ultimately be supported by measured latency, throughput, resource use, and recovery behavior. Table~\ref{tab:planned_system} defines these measurements without substituting interface functionality for performance evidence.

Published results in Table~\ref{tab:literature_comparison} show that richer RGB or multimodal inputs can achieve high accuracy, but they do not answer the same privacy-oriented skeleton-only question. Similarly, the difference between the published and reproduced TCNTE results is consistent with differences in archive size, validation selection, and evaluation partitions. The subject-disjoint comparison in Table~\ref{tab:primary_results}, the matched ablations, and the external Le2i test therefore provide the clearest evidence for choosing among the temporal models studied here.

\subsection{Outcome Against the Proposal}

The project preserves the proposal's care scenario and camera-to-cloud workflow, but the delivered scope differs in several concrete ways (Table~\ref{tab:goals}). RQ1 is partly addressed by learned-model comparisons; the geometry baseline was not benchmarked under the frozen protocol. RQ2 is addressed for model components, but not for the alert state machine's effect on real notifications. RQ3, the hardware and network performance question, remains open. Accordingly, the work constitutes an implemented prototype and completed offline study, not a validated unattended care system.
