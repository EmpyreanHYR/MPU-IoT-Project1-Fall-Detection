\section{Discussion}

The comparison distinguishes the temporal representation from its decision threshold and alert confirmation rules. MaskedBiMamba achieved higher precision at the common threshold and retained more performance under frame removal than TCNTE. TE favored clean-data sensitivity, and model-specific calibration could change the ranking of unmatched-event rates. Confirmation further reduced unmatched events while lowering event recall. Calibration for a monitoring setting therefore needs to address the complete decision pipeline.

The component ablations support retaining bidirectional processing and temporal attention in this architecture. Masking and quality pooling instead changed the sensitivity-precision balance without consistently improving clean F1. Their value depends on the observation conditions for which the classifier is calibrated. The combination extends masked temporal modeling~\cite{zhang2025fallmamba} to pose sequences. However, clean-input ablations do not isolate the component responsible for frame-loss tolerance; that attribution requires perturbation-specific ablations.

Local inference separates the availability of classification from that of network connectivity. Persistent records and acknowledgement after cloud storage preserved the event history through the tested interruptions. Hailo accelerated pose extraction, while the temporal classifier remained on the Pi CPU. Throughput alone does not establish timely fall detection, which also depends on window startup, valid poses, and alert confirmation.

Relative to the proposal, the final design moves temporal classification from the cloud to the Raspberry Pi and replaces MQTT/PostgreSQL with HTTP/SQLite. This change enables local inference and durable backlog delivery while retaining the planned cloud event history and dashboard. The proposed skeleton/geometry fusion developed into a temporal model with pose-quality features and component ablations. Offline replay and recovery tests verify the implemented data path; long-duration care monitoring remains outside the evaluated scope.
