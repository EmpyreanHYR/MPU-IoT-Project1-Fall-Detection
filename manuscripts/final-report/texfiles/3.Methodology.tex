\section{Temporal Detection Method}

\subsection{Data and Pose Caching}

The study uses CAUCAFall and GMDCSA-24 for the new subject-disjoint primary experiment and reserves Le2i for the TCNTE reproduction and cross-dataset evaluation, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. Le2i was introduced as a realistic single-camera fall-detection benchmark with changes in scene, viewpoint, clothing, illumination, shadow, and occlusion~\cite{charfi2012le2i}. The released annotated subset used by TCNTE is commonly reported as 191 videos (48 ADL and 143 fall videos), recorded at $320\times240$ pixels and 25 fps~\cite{yu2025tcnte}. The project copy was obtained from the Kaggle Le2i mirror and checked against the original dataUBFC catalogue entry~\cite{le2iKaggle2023,le2iDataUBFC2024}. It contained 190 decodable videos distributed over six recording-scene groups. Because reliable participant identifiers were absent from this copy, Le2i is not mixed into the subject-disjoint primary folds.

CAUCAFall was collected in an intentionally uncontrolled home environment. Ten participants each performed five fall types---forward, backward, left, right, and from sitting---and five ADLs---walking, hopping, picking up an object, sitting, and kneeling~\cite{guerrero2022caucafall}. We used the 100 videos in the official Mendeley Data version~5 release~\cite{caucafallDatasetV5}. They were recorded at $1080\times960$ pixels and 23 fps under natural, artificial, and night lighting, with variation in clothing, occlusion, background motion, fall angle, and camera distance. Subject folders are retained, enabling subject-disjoint folds. CAUCAFall additionally provides frame-level fall/no-fall and person-region labels, although this work uses the videos and temporal labels rather than training a frame detector.

GMDCSA-24 contributes 160 videos from four subject folders. The audited project copy contains 81 ADL videos and 79 fall videos, and every video passed a full decode audit before pose extraction. The dataset article is identified by DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2024.110892}{10.1016/j.dib.2024.110892}; the corresponding versioned dataset is available from \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.12921216}{Zenodo}~\cite{alam2024gmdcsa,gmdcsaDatasetV2}.

Temporal activity intervals for all three datasets came from the corresponding Le2i, CAUCAFall, and GMDCSA-24 label files in OmniFall~\cite{omnifallDataset2026}. Each label table was joined to local video paths through an explicit path map: all 190 Le2i, 100 CAUCAFall, and 160 GMDCSA-24 videos matched. The OmniFall download also contained 12,000 OF-Synthetic videos, but no synthetic video appears in a segment manifest, fold, or training run. Thus, OmniFall contributes annotation normalization only, not synthetic training samples, to the experiments in this paper.

The resulting 450 videos contain 967 Le2i, 258 CAUCAFall, and 458 GMDCSA-24 annotation segments. Pose extraction produced one validated cache per video: 450 unique cache records with finite arrays and consistent shapes. The positive label \emph{fall} denotes the dynamic transition; post-fall \emph{fallen} intervals are not merged into the positive class. A pretrained YOLOv8s-pose model~\cite{ultralyticsPose} with BoT-SORT tracking extracts one primary person track per video. Each cache stores timestamps, 17 COCO keypoints and their confidence values, a normalized person box, detector confidence, and the track identifier. Raw RGB frames are used only during this pose-extraction stage; the temporal classifiers train on cached pose arrays rather than the videos.

Only the 12 non-face body joints are used by the temporal classifiers. For each frame, coordinates are independently min--max normalized over the retained joints. The TCNTE reproduction uses $(x,y)$ and therefore has 24 input features per frame. The primary protocol additionally uses pose confidence, yielding 36 features. Three observation-quality features are retained at each time step: mean keypoint confidence, the proportion of joints with confidence at least 0.2, and person-box confidence.

\subsection{Window Construction and Data Splits}

Each window spans 1.0~s, consecutive windows begin 0.2~s apart, and every window is linearly resampled to 30 time steps. A window is positive when at least 50\% of its duration overlaps a fall segment. Windows with nonzero but sub-threshold overlap are discarded to avoid ambiguous transition labels. For videos containing a fall, all frozen protocols enable \texttt{exclude\_after\_fall}: windows beginning at or after the end of the last annotated fall transition are excluded. Consequently, prolonged post-fall lying is not evaluated as a positive event.

Two protocols are reported separately. The paper-aligned TCNTE reproduction and external benchmark use the 12,650 Le2i windows: 1,338 positive and 11,312 negative. The new primary protocol combines CAUCAFall and GMDCSA-24 in four subject-disjoint folds. CAUCAFall is grouped by its 10 subject identifiers and GMDCSA-24 by its four subject folders. Dataset names prefix every group identifier to prevent accidental collisions; every train, validation, and test partition contains both datasets, and no subject occurs in more than one partition within a fold.
The frozen primary manifest contains 7,869 windows: 1,371 positive and 6,498 negative. Each fold assigns the same window definitions to train, validation, and test only after group-level partitioning, so overlapping windows from one video never cross splits. Le2i is evaluated separately with all 12,650 windows marked as external test data; it is never used to fit the CAUCAFall--GMDCSA-24 models.

\subsection{Compared Temporal Models}

All models receive the same window manifest within a protocol. The TCN contains three residual temporal blocks with channels $[8,16,32]$, kernel size 5, dilations $1,2,4$, and dropout 0.2. The Transformer encoder (TE) linearly projects each frame to 32 dimensions, adds learned positional encoding, and applies one four-head encoder layer with a feed-forward width of 32. TCNTE passes the TCN sequence to the same encoder before temporal mean pooling.

The state-space baseline, UniMamba, projects each frame to 64 dimensions and uses two unidirectional Mamba layers with state size 16, convolution width 4, and expansion factor 2. Figure~\ref{fig:masked_bimamba} details the proposed MaskedBiMamba. During training, each frame is independently zeroed with probability 0.15, while at least one frame is retained. A 36-to-64 projection and learned positional encoding precede four-head temporal self-attention with a residual connection and layer normalization. Independent forward and reverse stacks each contain two residual Mamba layers (state size 16, convolution width 4, expansion 2). The reversed output is restored to the original time order; concatenated 128-dimensional features are projected to 64 dimensions and normalized. The three quality features are not zeroed by the training-time frame mask. A learned scalar gate $a_t$ is computed from the three-dimensional quality vector $\mathbf q_t$ and used for temporal aggregation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf z = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T} a_t\mathbf h_t}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_t},
    \qquad
    a_t = \max\!\left(10^{-6},\sigma(g(\mathbf q_t))\right).
\end{equation}
The quality network has widths $3\rightarrow32\rightarrow1$ with ReLU and sigmoid, and the final linear layer maps $\mathbf z$ from 64 dimensions to two logits. The softmax fall probability is used for evaluation. Masking is disabled at test time. Bidirectionality operates inside a complete buffered window; it does not establish frame-by-frame causal or zero-latency inference. This skeleton model is inspired by masked Mamba modeling but is not presented as an exact reproduction of the multimodal Fall-Mamba architecture.

\subsection{Optimization and Model Selection}

All models are trained for 100 epochs using Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch size 64, and weighted focal loss with $\gamma=3$. The TCNTE reproduction uses class weights $[1,30]$; the primary protocol estimates class weights from the training partition only. The checkpoint with the highest validation F1 is used for test evaluation. The reproduction uses seed 42; the primary comparison uses seeds 42, 3407, and 2026. CUDA mixed precision and deterministic seeds are enabled, while nondeterministic fused attention kernels are disabled.

After the primary matrix, ten MaskedBiMamba variants are retrained over the same four folds and three seeds, producing 120 ablation runs. The variants remove bidirectionality, temporal attention, quality-weighted pooling, frame masking, confidence input, or both confidence input and quality pooling; the frame-mask ratio is additionally set to 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, or 0.30 around the full model's value of 0.15. For robustness evaluation, the selected TCNTE and MaskedBiMamba checkpoints are tested without refitting under random joint removal, random frame removal, confidence noise at severities 0.1--0.3, and complete lower-body removal. Frame and joint removal independently zero observations with the stated probability before normalization; time steps remain in place, and frame removal also zeroes the corresponding box features. Confidence noise is Gaussian with standard deviation 0.1--0.3, followed by clipping to $[0,1]$, rather than a percentage of missing confidence values. The same checkpoints are then evaluated on the full Le2i manifest as an external test; no Le2i sample is used to fit the primary models.
