\section{Introduction}

Falls are short-duration events whose visual appearance may resemble daily activities such as sitting, kneeling, or lying down. Reliable camera-based detection therefore requires temporal reasoning rather than static posture classification. Indoor deployment introduces two further challenges: pose observations can become incomplete because of occlusion, lighting variation, or tracking failure, and continuously transmitting household RGB video creates avoidable privacy exposure.

Skeleton representations provide a compact motion signal and allow RGB frames to remain at the edge after pose extraction. TCNTE combines dilated temporal convolutions with a Transformer encoder for skeleton-based fall detection~\cite{yu2025tcnte}. However, conventional temporal classifiers generally treat observed frames as equally reliable even when keypoint confidence varies considerably. Mamba-based sequence modeling offers efficient long-range processing, but Fall-Mamba studies RGB and multimodal inputs rather than cached skeleton observations~\cite{zhang2025fallmamba}. The resulting research gap is both algorithmic and systemic: a practical fall detector must reason over unreliable skeleton sequences while connecting the camera, edge processor, cloud backend, and user-facing alert interface.

This work therefore investigates whether observation-quality information and bidirectional state-space modeling improve skeleton-only fall detection within a privacy-oriented edge--cloud architecture. A Raspberry Pi performs pose extraction near the camera, the cloud processes temporal pose windows, and an authenticated dashboard presents device state and alerts. The temporal classifier, MaskedBiMamba, combines forward and backward Mamba branches, temporal self-attention, training-time frame masking, and quality-weighted pooling derived from pose confidence.

The main contributions are: (1) an edge--cloud fall-detection prototype that transmits pose observations instead of continuous RGB video; (2) a quality-aware bidirectional Mamba architecture for skeleton sequences; and (3) a controlled comparison with TCN, Transformer, TCNTE, and UniMamba using both window-level and video/event-level metrics. The report also defines the remaining component-ablation, robustness, and system-latency experiments without assigning results before they are measured.
