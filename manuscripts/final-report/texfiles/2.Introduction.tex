\section{Introduction}

Falls can resemble sitting, kneeling, or lying down when viewed in a single frame. Their distinction depends on the descent and subsequent posture. Camera-based monitoring captures this motion without a wearable device, but occlusion and tracking failures can interrupt pose observations. Skeleton sequences provide a compact motion representation, yet missing observations can alter the evidence available to a temporal classifier.

The intended setting is an indoor assisted-living space, where caregivers review suspected falls and acknowledge events through a monitoring interface. The design objectives are to process video near the camera, continue detection during network outages, and preserve event times when records reach the cloud after reconnection.

Temporal classifiers relate pose estimates across frames rather than treating each frame independently. Yu et al.~\cite{yu2025tcnte} combined dilated temporal convolutions, a Transformer encoder, and weighted focal loss in TCNTE, with deployment on a Jetson device. Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025yosap} combined tracking, AlphaPose, and CNN-LSTM classification in YOSAP-LSTM for construction scenes with occlusion. These systems connect temporal classification to the constraints of an edge processing pipeline.

Observation quality is also relevant to the temporal representation. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2025fallmamba} combined video-audio fusion, temporal attention, and frame masking in Fall-Mamba. Chun et al.~\cite{chun2025stonegat} incorporated skeleton geometry and confidence information into StoneGAT, with node-feature removal during training. Building on these approaches, we examine whether combining temporal context and pose quality preserves classification performance when extracted observations are interrupted.

Our model contribution is MaskedBiMamba: independent forward and backward Mamba branches~\cite{gu2023mamba} combine temporal attention, training-time frame masking, and quality-weighted pooling. The two directions relate a descent to its subsequent posture within a buffered one-second window. Matched temporal baselines, component ablations, and pose perturbations demonstrate the model's precision and frame-loss tolerance. Window, video, event, and confirmed-alert metrics cover successive stages of the decision pipeline.

Our system contribution is FallGuard, which brings this classifier to a Raspberry Pi with Hailo-accelerated pose extraction, persistent local alert records, and an authenticated monitoring workspace. Classification continues during disconnection, and deferred cloud synchronization preserves each record's identity and capture time. Hardware replay and recovery tests connect the model evaluation to a working camera--edge--cloud--user workflow.
