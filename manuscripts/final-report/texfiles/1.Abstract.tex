\begin{abstract}
Camera-based fall detection requires temporal reasoning while remaining robust to incomplete pose observations and limiting the transmission of identifiable video. This project develops an edge--cloud system in which a Raspberry Pi extracts human keypoints near the camera and sends pose observations without direct identity fields to a cloud service for temporal inference, alert management, event storage, and dashboard visualization. We further propose MaskedBiMamba, a skeleton classifier that combines bidirectional Mamba branches, temporal self-attention, training-time frame masking, and pose-quality-aware aggregation. The primary subject-disjoint evaluation contains 60 runs over CAUCAFall and GMDCSA-24. The Transformer encoder obtains the highest window F1 ($0.6581\pm0.0766$) and video F1 ($0.7686\pm0.0257$), whereas MaskedBiMamba gives the highest window precision ($0.6048\pm0.0856$) and the lowest offline false-alarm rate ($198.5\pm40.8$~h$^{-1}$). Under 30\% random frame loss, MaskedBiMamba retains a window F1 of $0.6276\pm0.0507$, compared with $0.4361\pm0.0669$ for TCNTE. On the external Le2i set, the two models obtain similar clean video F1 (0.8299 and 0.8307, respectively). Ablations support the contribution of bidirectionality and temporal attention, but also show that frame masking and quality pooling are not uniformly beneficial under the fixed operating point. These alarm rates are measured per evaluated video hour, not per hour of continuous non-fall monitoring. Edge--cloud latency and power have not been measured in a controlled deployment trial.
\end{abstract}
