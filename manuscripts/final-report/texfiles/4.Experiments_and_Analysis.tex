\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setting}

The positive class is \emph{fall}. Window accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 use threshold 0.5; AUROC uses the continuous probabilities. A video is predicted as positive when any window reaches this threshold. For event metrics, overlapping positive target windows are merged into reference intervals; positive predicted windows are merged likewise and matched one-to-one by overlap. Event recall is the detected fraction of these window-derived reference events. False alarms are unmatched predicted intervals divided by the sum, over evaluated videos, of $(\max t_{\rm end}-\min t_{\rm start})/3600$. Thus, all reported false alarms/h refer to evaluated video time, including fall videos, not negative-only exposure or deployed alert counts. Delay is the mean matched interval-onset difference, can be negative, and excludes buffering, pose extraction, communication, and alert confirmation. Tables~\ref{tab:environment} and~\ref{tab:configuration} summarize the execution environment and evaluation protocols. The frozen CAUCAFall--GMDCSA-24 primary protocol evaluates every model over four subject-disjoint folds and three random seeds.

\subsection{CAUCAFall--GMDCSA-24 Primary Experiment}

The primary manifest contains 260 real videos, 716 annotated activity segments, and 7,869 fixed windows (1,371 fall and 6,498 non-fall). The four folds use 14 dataset-prefixed subject groups, and every split contains both source datasets without subject overlap. Pose extraction completed for all 450 videos in the project, including the 190 Le2i videos reserved for reproduction and external testing. The formal matrix comprises five models, four folds, and three seeds (60 runs), each trained for the frozen 100 epochs. All expected \texttt{metrics.json} files were present and no failed run was recorded.

Table~\ref{tab:primary_results} shows that the Transformer encoder (TE) obtains the highest window F1, AUROC, video F1, and event recall. MaskedBiMamba obtains the highest window accuracy and precision and the lowest false-alarm rate. Its video F1 is close to TE ($0.7632$ versus $0.7686$), but the fixed threshold still produces approximately 198 unmatched predicted intervals per evaluated video hour. The result therefore supports a precision--alarm-rate advantage rather than a universal accuracy advantage.

\subsection{Mechanism Ablations and Robustness Tests}

Ten modified MaskedBiMamba variants were retrained over all four folds and three seeds, for 120 additional runs. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares them with the full model from the primary matrix. Removing temporal attention or bidirectionality reduces both window and video F1 and increases false alarms. However, the full combination is not uniformly optimal: removing frame masking gives the highest window F1, removing quality pooling gives the highest video F1, and mask ratios 0.05, 0.20, and 0.30 reduce the mean false-alarm rate relative to 0.15; ratio 0.10 increases it to 206.0~h$^{-1}$. Confidence input improves video F1 and false-alarm rate relative to its removal, even though the variant without confidence has slightly higher window F1. These descriptive results indicate component trade-offs and do not establish statistical significance.

\subsection{External Le2i Evaluation}

The 24 primary MaskedBiMamba and TCNTE checkpoints were evaluated on all 12,650 Le2i windows without external-data fitting. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:external}, clean performance is nearly tied at the video level, while TCNTE has higher clean window F1. MaskedBiMamba is substantially more stable under random frame loss: at 30\% frame drop it retains video F1 $0.8243$ and window F1 $0.4507$, compared with $0.8126$ and $0.2782$ for TCNTE. Both models remain vulnerable to complete lower-body removal and random joint loss at the window level. Under the evaluated window labels, Le2i contains 130 positive and 60 negative videos: an all-positive predictor already obtains video F1 $2(130)/(2(130)+60)=0.8125$. Clean video specificity is only 0.1958 for MaskedBiMamba and 0.1736 for TCNTE. Thus, video F1 near 0.83 alone does not demonstrate strong cross-dataset discrimination.
